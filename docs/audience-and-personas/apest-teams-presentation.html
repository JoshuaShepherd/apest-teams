02 / 30 THE QUESTION
BASELINE PROPOSAL

Three conclusions

Conclusion 01

The pricing anchor problem is worse than we accounted for.

The 5Q assessment sells for $10. Working Genius, the nearest secular analogue, sells for $25 and includes the team map. We proposed charging $100 per person per year for interpretation on top of a $10 measurement. Every buyer will do that arithmetic out loud.

Conclusion 02

Price the team, not the seat.

Flat banded team pricing removes seat counting, removes the incentive to exclude a marginal person, makes added seats free so they generate assessment volume, and yields a higher realized revenue per seat than $100.

Conclusion 03

What decides this business is the 5Q relationship.

It is a political question rather than a commercial one. Alan is on both sides. Access was never in doubt. What is in doubt is whether an arrangement Alan says yes to is one he can defend in a room he is not standing in.

05 / 30 PRICING RECOMMENDATION
APEST TEAMS BANDS

Five reasons, and one cost

It kills the $10 comparison. A team price is not divisible by an assessment price in the buyer's head. They compare $1,250 to a consultant, a retreat, or a staff development line.
It removes the exclusion incentive. Under per-seat, adding the part-time youth director costs $100 and someone decides she is not worth it. That decision is corrosive to a product whose thesis is that the team is a system.
It generates assessment volume. Free marginal seats mean teams add people, which means people take 5Q.
It raises realized revenue per seat. Small teams pay a floor, and that is where the volume is.
It matches how churches budget. A fixed line item is approvable. A line item that changes when someone is hired generates a support ticket.
The cost: per-seat monetizes headcount growth automatically. Banding steps instead. In a church staff, headcount growth is slow and the friction of counting is high, so stepping is the better trade.

06 / 30 PRICING MECHANICS
REALIZED REVENUE ECONOMICS

Four jobs the subscription has to do

The Living Team Map
The current team, not a PDF of the team six months ago. Composition, concentrations, absences, distribution against population benchmarks, changes over time.
The Agentic Interpreter
Conversation with the team's own configuration, bound to individual data, team history, organizational context, and Alan's corpus. Hedged language. A mirror rather than an oracle.
Organizational Memory
What the team noticed, asked, attempted, restructured, decided. The team does not start from zero at every annual retreat.
Change Detection
Composition and role changes made visible, with the questions those changes reopen. Sparing, not weekly.
07 / 30 PRODUCT SPECIFICATION
CORE CAPABILITIES

Three additions

The organization layer

We specified a team product and priced it as an organizational business. A church with a nine-person staff also has an elder board, a worship team, a youth team, a planting cohort. The question that matters to the executive pastor is not what the leadership team's configuration is. It is that the staff is Shepherd-heavy, the elder board is Teacher-heavy, there is no Apostolic capacity anywhere in the building, and the two most Evangelistic people are volunteers with no decision rights. That view justifies an organizational price and makes expansion natural.

Named rituals

"The mirror stays on" is a good phrase and a bad operating model. Software that is always available with no scheduled moment gets used once and abandoned.

Usability at partial data

In a nine-person team, three people will not have 5Q data on the day of purchase. If the product is inert until all nine connect, activation dies in week two.

08 / 30 PRODUCT GAPS
ARCHITECTURE EXTENSIONS

Three moments, and only three

Trigger: On first sufficient data

The Configuration Review

First team map, first questions, saved baseline. The only one guaranteed to happen, so it has to be extraordinary.

Trigger: Material change

The Change Review

Composition, role, or assessment changes, and the questions they reopen. Sparing. A review that fires for a job-title edit trains people to ignore it.

Trigger: Annual planning season

The Annual Reading

The team's own planning season, not the subscription anniversary. Returns them to what they saw, tried, and learned. This is the renewal event.

11 / 30 CUSTOMER JOURNEY
ANNUAL LIFECYCLE

$500, twenty minutes, eight a month

The proposal was $250 per team with up to an hour of Alan's time, priced attractively because its existence increases trust in the whole environment.

The trust function is right. It is also the argument for raising the price.

That function is performed by the offer existing and being purchasable, not by the number of units sold. At $250, demand passes 96 units a year once there are a few hundred teams, and we run a permanent waitlist. A permanent waitlist does not read as "Alan is real and reachable." It reads as "Alan is not available," which damages comprehension more than the higher price does. The increase is what keeps the offer purchasable.

Twenty minutes, not sixty. The system prepares the briefing. Alan reads for five, records for ten, five in buffer. If the briefing needs an hour of his attention, the briefing is not good enough. Making the briefing excellent is a software problem solved once. An hour of Alan is a cost paid every time.

13 / 30 CAPACITY MODELING
FOUNDER BOTTLENECK ANALYSIS

Price the human higher

A certified APEST practitioner reviews the team and runs a live sixty-minute session. $750. Priced above Alan's review, deliberately.

The inversion is the point and buyers understand it instantly. Alan's review is cheap and scarce because it is Alan and it is recorded. The practitioner review is expensive and available because it is a live hour of a trained human's time with your actual team.

This makes the ontology of the whole environment unmistakable, which is what the $250 price was originally meant to do. It solves the capacity problem without ever telling a customer that Alan is unavailable. And it gives the 5Q certified practitioner network a product they own and make margin on, which matters for reasons the deck returns to on screen 25.

14 / 30 TIER INVERSION
PRACTITIONER NETWORK TIERS

$499 holds. The bundle does not.

$499 is right for a facilitator-led eight-week cohort. It sits inside the $300 to $900 band the network pricing strategy already established. An Alan-led cohort could carry $900 and should be rare, at most twice a year, treated as marketing.

The proposal was to include the first year of APEST Teams inside the $499 for participants on an eligible team. Do not.

It creates an accounting problem at exactly the renewal conversation you cannot afford one at. Twelve months later the team leader asks why the invoice is $1,250 when last year was free, and the answer involves explaining that it was never free, it was inside someone's tuition. That conversation costs more than $100 of margin.

The Recommended Replacement

Formation includes twelve months of individual APEST access. The person's own profile, their own agent, their own history. Not team access. Near-zero marginal cost, genuinely valuable.

If their organization already subscribes to Teams, note that the access is covered and discount nothing.

Organizations enrolling four or more people in one cohort get a year of Teams at the banded price with the first quarter credited. Legible, real, and attached to the organization rather than to an individual's tuition.

This inverts the relationship. Formation stops being a product that gives away Teams and becomes the best lead generator Teams has.

16 / 30 UNIT ECONOMICS
COHORT MARGIN & FACILITATOR PIPELINE

The recurring-value test, run honestly

For a nine-person church staff, the probability that at least one person joins or leaves in twelve months is 60 to 75 percent. So change detection fires for most teams most years.

But change firing is not value. A notification that Maya joined is interesting for ninety seconds. For a stable team in a stable year, two or three consequential moments will not occur.

Four things drive renewal, in descending strength.

Annual is right. But annual has to be earned by the ritual structure, not asserted by the billing system.

17 / 30 RENEWAL DYNAMICS
CHURN RESISTANCE & VALUE HOOKS

The CAC constraint decides everything

At $1,250 average contract value and 80 percent gross retention, lifetime gross profit per team is $4,000 to $4,800. A healthy acquisition cost is therefore $400 to $800.

That number does not fund outbound sales development. It does not fund paid acquisition at church-vertical rates. It barely funds a single human demo call.

So the motion is forced.

Sell on the calendar. Church budgets are set annually, often in autumn for a January start. Concentrate acquisition August through November and model the seasonality, which all three forecasts quietly smooth.

24 / 30 FINANCIAL PROJECTIONS
FULL BASE CASE MODEL

This is political, not financial

Alan authored the framework, Alan is behind 5Q, and Alan is a co-founder here. Access was never the variable. Alan decides.

What is in question is whether an arrangement Alan says yes to is one he can defend in a room he is not standing in. The 5Q team's loyalty runs to Alan rather than to Movemental. An agreement they experience as extraction does not produce a blocked product. It produces a referral placement that never gets built, a bulk redemption path that stays in the backlog, and a practitioner network telling its clients this is software trying to replace them.

Contractual access with unenthusiastic operators is a right you cannot use.

The individual assessment is not at risk

APEST Teams drives it hard. This is the easy part of the conversation and it should be said first.

The organizational 5Q profile is at risk

5Q Central already sells a personal or organizational APEST profile. That is the one surface where APEST Teams would take revenue directly off their page. Decide it explicitly rather than letting feature drift decide it. Either their organizational assessment becomes an input to APEST Teams, or it stays theirs and Teams routes people to it.

Training, coaching, and the certified practitioners are at risk

A team that can interrogate its configuration for a year buys fewer webinars. And software that interprets a team looks, to a practitioner, like software doing their job. This is where the complaint comes from, and these are the people who carry 5Q's credibility in the field.

27 / 30 FAILURE MODES
TEN STRUCTURAL RISKS

Product specification, not compliance appendix

The software helps a team see and discuss patterns. It does not turn a framework into an algorithmic authority over people.
Affirmative join. An individual joins a team by their own affirmative act. A leader can invite. A leader cannot import.
No third-party upload. No leader may upload someone else's assessment report. Structurally impossible, not merely prohibited.
Departure is real. Named data leaves the active model. Historical states retain the position without the person: a Shepherd-primary member departed in March 2026.
Refusal class in code. The agent has a refusal class implemented in code. It refuses to rank named individuals for termination, promotion, compensation, hiring, or performance. It redirects rather than lectures.

29 / 30 NINETY-DAY PLAN
SEQUENCED EXECUTION TIMELINE

How large, and what we decide today

As a standalone product in the English-language North American church and mission-nonprofit market, APEST Teams can reasonably reach $2M to $4M of ARR in four years without a partnership breakthrough, and $6M to $10M with a real 5Q agreement and denominational channels. Above roughly $15M it runs out of addressable organizations and needs international replication or a materially different average contract value.

That is a good business. It is not the most important thing about it.

APEST Teams would be the first Movemental product that sells to an organization rather than to a leader, at a recurring price, with a defined onboarding, a defined renewal event, and a defined expansion path. Everything else in the portfolio is a leader's own product, a services engagement, or a platform build. This is the first one with software economics and a repeatable motion. If it works, that capability transfers to the Neighborhood Intelligence Report, to the Charter, and to the whole module library.

There is no revenue yet, which means nobody is defending a number, which means the 5Q conversation can still be about what is fair rather than about what is owed. That is the reason for sixty days, and it is the only reason.
Four Decisions Leaving the Room
One. Price per team, not per person.
$750 / $1,250 / $2,000 / $6,000 flat bands. Kills the $10 reference comparison and removes seat exclusion.
Two. Alan's Review at $500 with a hard monthly cap.

Three. The 5Q protective proposal sequence.
Assessment to $25. Royalty to 5Q. Bundle remitted at cost. Practitioner Review owned by network.
